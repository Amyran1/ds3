F01. YoY drift / cohort-aging decomposition

YoY drift (7.8%→12.5%) is 198% cohort aging: each cohort enters at ~8% and matures. Harness MUST use temporal holdout.

Wave 1BLOCKINGconfounder

Question

Pre-EDA showed YoY drift is empirically cohort aging. Formal verification: pooled AR 7.80% → 12.51% with 198% explained by cohort aging. Harness must use cohort/tenure-aware temporal validation; random row splits and pooled-baseline comparisons are invalid.

In plain English

Two coffee shops each have 100 customers. Shop A opened in 2024; half its 2024 customers were one-time tourists who never came back. By 2026, only A's 50 loyal regulars remain, and they buy way more per visit. Shop B opened in 2026 with 100 fresh customers, some of whom haven't bought yet.

Shop A's per-visit sales now look way better than B's — but A didn't make better coffee. It just kept its highest-intent customers. Civic Shout's drift (7.8% → 12.5%) looks like product improvement but is almost entirely the same dynamic: the 2024 cohort who didn't unsubscribe are getting more loyal, while newer cohorts enter at the same ~7-8% they always did. F01 proves this with a cohort-by-cohort plot, which forces the harness to split data by send DATE not random rows.

Method

lf = ue_cache.scan(2)
first_send = lf.group_by("user_id").agg(pl.col("date_sent").min().alias("first_send_date"))
enriched = lf.join(first_send, on="user_id").with_columns(
    pl.col("first_send_date").dt.year().alias("cohort_year"),
    pl.col("date_sent").dt.year().alias("calendar_year"),
    pl.col("date_sent").dt.quarter().alias("calendar_quarter"),
)
cohort_qtr = (
    enriched.group_by(["cohort_year", "calendar_year", "calendar_quarter"])
    .agg(pl.col("actioned").mean().alias("action_rate"), pl.len().alias("n_sends"))
    .sort(["cohort_year", "calendar_year", "calendar_quarter"])
    .collect()
)

Impact on harness / features

**Aging explains 198% of YoY drift.** Pooled AR moved 7.80% → 12.51% (2024Q2→2026Q2). The 2024 cohort alone moved 7.80% → 17.13% over the same period — matching the pooled drift magnitude.

**Split convention**: temporal holdout by send date with tenure-bin stratification. Random row splits inflate AUC by leaking cohort composition.

**Baseline**: the pooled 9.87% target is mechanically suppressed by early-cohort under-representation. Per-cohort or per-tenure-bucket targets are more honest. The 2026 cohort baseline (~7-8% entry AR) is the cleanest single comparison point.
